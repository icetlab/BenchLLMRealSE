The original code's logic for calculating the minimum deletions is complex and inefficient. It uses a sliding window approach with nested loops that repeatedly calculate sums, resulting in a high time complexity, roughly O(U^3) where U is the number of unique characters.

The improvement replaces this convoluted logic with a much simpler and more performant O(U^2) algorithm. The new approach iterates through each unique frequency from the sorted list, considers it as the minimum frequency to keep, and calculates the total deletions required for this scenario. The total deletions are the sum of two costs:
1. Deleting all characters with frequencies smaller than the chosen minimum.
2. Trimming characters with frequencies larger than `minimum + k` down to this maximum allowed frequency.

A running prefix sum is used to efficiently calculate the first part of the cost. This new implementation is not only faster but also significantly more readable and maintainable.
