The original solution has a time complexity of approximately O(N^3) due to three nested loops that iterate through all possible subarrays of lengths between `l` and `r` and calculate their sums. This is highly inefficient for larger inputs.

My approach improves the performance to O(N) by using a combination of prefix sums and a sliding window technique with a deque:
1.  First, I compute a prefix sum array. This allows calculating the sum of any subarray in O(1) time, reducing the problem complexity to O(N^2).
2.  To further optimize, the problem of finding the minimum subarray sum `prefixSum[j] - prefixSum[i]` for a fixed endpoint `j` is transformed into `prefixSum[j] - max(prefixSum[i])` over a sliding window of start indices `i`.
3.  I use a deque (double-ended queue) to efficiently find the maximum prefix sum within this sliding window in amortized O(1) time per step. I also use `long` for prefix sums to prevent potential integer overflow.

This combination of techniques reduces the overall time complexity to O(N) and uses O(N) space for the prefix sums and the deque, which is a significant improvement.
