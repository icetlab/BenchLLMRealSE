The original solution's primary performance bottleneck lies in its method for finding "beautiful indices". After efficiently locating all occurrences of substrings `a` and `b` using the KMP algorithm, it employs a nested loop to compare every occurrence of `a` against every occurrence of `b`. This leads to a time complexity of O(N*M) for this checking phase, where N and M are the respective counts of occurrences. In scenarios where `a` and `b` appear frequently, this can approach a quadratic complexity relative to the length of the input string `s`.

My improvement replaces this inefficient nested loop with a linear-time two-pointer algorithm. Since the lists of indices for `a` and `b` are inherently sorted, we can iterate through both lists simultaneously with two pointers. For each index of `a`, we advance the pointer for `b` just enough to find a valid corresponding index within the specified distance `k`. This avoids redundant comparisons and reduces the complexity of this critical step from O(N*M) to O(N+M), resulting in a much faster solution.
