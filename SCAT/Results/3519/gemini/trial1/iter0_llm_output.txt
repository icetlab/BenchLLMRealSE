Second, it iterates through all possible players from 0 to `n-1`, and for each player, it checks their item counts to see if they meet the winning condition. This can be inefficient, especially when `n` is large, as it requires a second loop that is dependent on `n`, not just the input size.

My improvement refactors the logic into a single pass over the `pick` array. As each pick is processed, the count for that player and item is updated. Immediately after updating, a check is performed to see if the player has just met their winning condition (picking an item `player_id + 1` times). A `HashSet` is used to keep track of the winning players, which efficiently handles duplicates. Once a player is confirmed as a winner, an optimization skips processing any of their subsequent picks. This single-pass algorithm reduces the time complexity from O(P + n) to O(P), where P is the number of picks, providing a significant performance boost for cases with a large `n`.
