The original solution has a time complexity of O(N^3). It iterates through all pairs of points `(i, j)` and for each pair, it performs a check that involves iterating through all `n` points again. This is inefficient.

My improvement reduces the time complexity to O(N^2). The key ideas are:
1.  **Change Sorting Strategy:** Instead of sorting by the y-coordinate, I sort the points primarily by their x-coordinate (ascending) and secondarily by their y-coordinate (descending). This ordering is crucial because for any two points `p_i` and `p_j` with `i < j` in the sorted array, we are guaranteed that `p_i.x <= p_j.x`.
2.  **Simplify Conditions:** With this new sort order, a point `p_i` can only be an "upper-left" point for a subsequent point `p_j` (where `j > i`). The condition for this is `p_i.y >= p_j.y`. Furthermore, any potential "blocking" point `p_k` must lie between `p_i` and `p_j` in the sorted array (`i < k < j`).
3.  **Efficient Inner Loop:** For a fixed `p_i`, as we iterate through potential `p_j`'s, we can efficiently check for blocking points. Instead of a nested loop, we maintain a running maximum of the y-coordinates of intermediate points that could block the pair. This allows us to check the validity condition in O(1) time, bringing the total complexity down from O(N^3) to O(N^2).

This new approach eliminates the separate `isValidPair` method and replaces the triple-nested loop structure with a more efficient double-nested loop.
